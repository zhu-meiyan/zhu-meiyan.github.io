01 / THE PROJECT

What I was working on.

The broader research effort focused on a high-throughput polymer formulation platform. The robotic arm was intended to help move vials out of the workspace so they would not interfere with later experiments.

My part of the project centered on getting the arm's electronics and control system working. I worked with an Arduino Mega, stepper motors and drivers, limit switches, joystick and potentiometer inputs, and a servo-operated gripper.

01 · CONTROLLERArduino Mega running the control logic.
02 · MOTIONNEMA-17 stepper motors with TB6600 drivers.
03 · INPUTSJoysticks, switches, and potentiometers for manual control.
04 · SAFETYLimit switches used to stop motion in a restricted direction.
02 / MY CONTRIBUTIONS

Making the pieces work together.

I worked on wiring and integrating the electronics, then wrote and adapted Arduino control code to operate the motors and gripper. The system included manual controls as well as a basic preset sequence for moving between pickup, drop-off, and home positions.

I also worked on inverse-kinematics calculations to translate target positions into motor movement. This involved converting the calculated angles and distances into stepper-motor steps.

WHAT I LEARNEDGetting a formula into code is one thing. Making it match the real dimensions and movement of a physical arm is another.
DETAIL I HAD TO THINK THROUGHLimit switches needed to stop movement toward the limit without preventing the arm from moving away from it.
03 / TESTING + ITERATION

Calibration was the hard part.

One of the biggest challenges was calibrating the motors. The movement commanded in software did not always correspond exactly to the physical distance or angle traveled. I tested the arm, compared commanded and measured movement, and adjusted the steps-per-degree and steps-per-millimeter values.

This process helped me understand how mechanical tolerances, motor behavior, and software assumptions affect one another. It also made testing and measurement a much bigger part of the work than I had expected.

04 / REFLECTION

What I'd take forward.

I'm proud that I helped bring the electronics and control code together into a prototype that could be operated manually and run a basic preset transfer sequence. I also came away with a better appreciation for how much iteration goes into making a physical system behave reliably.

If I continued developing the project, I'd want to improve the mechanical robustness, expand the safety checks, and explore more complete integration with the surrounding platform. Those are areas I'd approach with a better understanding of the system's limitations after working through the first prototype.
